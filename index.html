India’s merchandise trade expanded in July 2026, with both imports and exports showing changes in value across major commodity groups and trading partners. Trade data for July 2025 to July 2026, released by the Department of Commerce on September 15, reveals key trends.

India’s merchandise trade data show total merchandise imports rose from $64.86 billion to $76.22 billion, a 17.52% increase across the period. The largest import category continued to be petroleum, crude, and products, which rose by almost 18% from around $15.5 billion to $18.3 billion. Electronics imports rose by about 46% a year, making them one of the fastest-growing major groups in the import basket.

Mechanical, electrical and non-electrical equipment rose from around $5.4 billion to $6.1 billion, a 13% increase, while gold increased from $4.0 billion to $4.2 billion, or nearly 4%. Imports of petroleum and crude oil surged, while petroleum product exports rose by $10 billion.

India’s exports rose 19.63% in July 2026, with petroleum products and electronic goods among the fastest-growing major categories, according to the data. Petroleum product exports increased 67.64%, from $4.13 billion in July 2025 to $6.92 billion in July 2026. Electronic goods exports rose 57.40%, from $3.76 billion to $5.92 billion.

Engineering goods remained the largest export category in the July data, rising 17.71% to $12.24 billion from $10.40 billion a year earlier. Organic and inorganic chemical exports expanded 14.39% to $2.80 billion, while drugs and pharmaceuticals rose marginally by 0.74% to $2.69 billion. Other categories also recorded increases. Plastic and linoleum exports rose 11.01% to $863 million, while cotton yarn, fabrics and made-ups increased 8.40% to $1.11 billion.

Growth was not uniform across the export basket. Gems and jewellery exports fell 2.71% to $2.32 billion, while ready-made garment exports declined 4.48% to $1.28 billion. Rice exports fell 6.60% to $860 million.

India’s exports also increased across several major trading partners in July 2026. The United States remained India’s largest export destination, with exports rising from about $8.0 billion in July 2025 to $9.0 billion in July 2026, an increase of roughly 13%. Exports to the UAE rose from about $3.0 billion to $3.3 billion, while exports to Singapore jumped from around $0.9 billion to $1.6 billion, an increase of 84%.

Exports to China rose from about $1.3 billion to $2.2 billion, an increase of roughly 65%. Several other markets also recorded increases, including Japan, Malaysia, South Africa and Hong Kong. However, exports to the UK fell by about 4%, while exports to Bangladesh also declined.

Taken together, the data show that India’s merchandise trade is changing on both sides of the trade balance. The figures measure trade value, however, and do not by themselves show whether changes were driven by higher volumes, prices or a combination of the two. Imports increased largely because of higher values across major commodity groups, particularly petroleum and electronics, while exports saw especially strong growth in petroleum products, electronic goods and engineering goods.

At the country level, the United States remained India’s largest export market, although several Asian and European markets also recorded increases. The data point to a shift toward petroleum products, electronic goods and engineering goods in India’s merchandise trade. On the import side, petroleum and electronics recorded some of the largest increases, while on the export side, petroleum products and electronic goods grew faster than several traditional categories.
